Yatra Freight Tracker

A B2B freight forwarding platform for FCL, LCL and air shipments — cargo tracking, carrier coordination, invoicing, and freight documentation for Yatra Online Limited.

Challenge

Freight logistics means keeping shipment status, financial records, and government compliance in sync across FCL, LCL and air freight — each with its own document flow, and each touching internal accounting and a government e-invoicing system that had to be reconciled correctly, not just quickly. Reporting needs kept growing in complexity as the business added more data sources to track.

Approach

I took part in architecture, design, and product sessions rather than working from a finished spec — pushing for a dedicated financial microservice instead of extending the existing monolith further, and for a saga-pattern approach to keep multi-step financial transactions consistent without a single point of failure.

Technical solution

I architected and built the financial microservice for invoicing and transaction management, led the integration with the government e-invoicing system and internal accounting tools, and built a reporting microservice with Angular-based visualization that aggregates data from multiple databases into a centralized warehouse — with output to email and Google Sheets. I also introduced Kong API Gateway for centralized routing, rate limiting and authentication, added Redis caching to cut response times, led the migration from self-hosted MongoDB to MongoDB Atlas, and set up Graylog for centralized logging so I'd have real observability into how each service was behaving in production, not just whether it was up or down.

Outcome

The financial microservice runs with far fewer reconciliation errors and at a noticeably lower processing cost than the workflow it replaced, while handling a higher volume of transactions. The Atlas migration and platform-wide performance work brought response times down meaningfully across the product. Day to day, I lead a team of 5 engineers on this codebase — sprint planning, code review, and task estimation against actual delivery.

Stack

Node.js · Express.js · Angular · MongoDB (Atlas) · Redis · Kong API Gateway · RabbitMQ / RSMQ · Kafka · AWS (EC2, S3) · Docker · Nginx · Graylog (observability)

Secondary case study · Code Brew Labs

Royo Apps — configurable SaaS platform

A single SaaS codebase configured to run three different business models — food delivery, e-commerce, and rentals — rather than three separate builds.

E-commerce

Challenge

Food delivery, e-commerce, and rental businesses share a lot of application structure — accounts, listings, checkout, payments — but differ enough in workflow and terminology that a naive shared codebase turns into an if/else maze fast.

Approach

I led frontend development and built configuration-based strategies so each business vertical could be expressed as configuration rather than a fork of the codebase — keeping one platform adaptable across all three models.

Technical solution

Beyond the configuration layer, I integrated more than 20 payment gateways to support the range of markets and business types the platform served, and optimized the existing codebase for performance as the platform's surface area grew.

Outcome

User engagement improved as a result of the frontend and performance work, and the configuration-based approach let the platform support new business models without a rebuild. I also owned task estimation and delivery timing for this work.
